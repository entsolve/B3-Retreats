This is a real photograph of a real woman. Retouch it: keep HER, replace the whole city around her.
It must still be unmistakably the same person, photographed on the same day, out in the countryside.
If a stranger who knows her looked at the result, they must not hesitate for a second.

HER FACE — copy it, do not interpret it. Treat the head as a region that is transferred pixel for
pixel and only relit. Same face shape and jawline, same cheekbones, same chin, same nose. Same
strong, dark, straight eyebrows with their exact thickness and arch — do not thin them, do not lift
them. Same eyes, same lids, same lashes, same direction of gaze off to her right. Same mouth in the
SAME expression: lips closed in a small amused smile, corners slightly raised — NO open mouth, NO
visible teeth, NO grin, NO laugh. Same freckles across nose, cheeks and shoulders, in the same
places and the same density. Same skin with pores, fine lines and uneven tone; same age. Do not
slim the face, do not smooth, do not beautify, do not symmetrise, do not rejuvenate, do not add
make-up, do not enlarge the eyes, do not lengthen the neck.

KEEP AS THEY ARE — her dark-blonde hair pulled straight back into the low bun with the brown satin
scrunchie and the loose strands at the temple; the sunglasses pushed up on her head, resting ABOVE
her eyes on her hair and never covering them; the fine chain with the small dark pendant; the
champagne-coloured satin slip dress with the cowl neck and the thin straps; the beaded bracelet and
the steel-and-gold watch. Keep her head angle, the tilt of her upper body, her shoulders and the
framing of the original.

BACKGROUND — replace the concrete stairs, the steel handrails, the grey wall and the pale ceiling
panels completely. Nothing man-made stays in the frame. Behind her: a wide harvested wheat field of
dusty gold running up to a low hill, a long dark-green line of oaks and hazels along the top, dry
seed heads and pale wild grasses, a hazy grey-blue late-summer sky. Central German hill country in
August, late afternoon. It sits far behind her and is thrown well out of focus — 85 mm at f/2.2,
shapes and colour only, no readable detail, soft round highlights in the dry grasses.

HER ARMS — she was leaning on a handrail with both hands; the handrail is gone. Both arms now hang
down and away from her body at the same angles they already have. Her hands are the part that goes
wrong most easily, so build them slowly: two hands, five separate fingers each, normal human finger
length — not elongated, not tapering to points, not fused. Each hand is unmistakably attached to a
forearm that runs to an elbow and a shoulder of hers. The lower hand brushes through the tops of the
dry grasses; the other simply hangs open and empty. If a hand cannot be drawn cleanly, let the tall
grasses cover it rather than inventing fingers.

LIGHT — one low sun, roughly 25° above the horizon, from behind her right shoulder. A warm rim along
her shoulder and the edge of the bun; her face in soft open shade, filled by bounce from the bright
field. Relight her whole body so it agrees with the new background: the flat grey city light is gone,
the satin picks up warm gold where it catches the sun and stays cool in the folds. The cowl neck
keeps its heavy, readable fold — no flat painted fabric, no smeared straps.

COLOUR — muted, restrained late-summer palette: dusty gold, oat, warm sand, deep green, cool
grey-blue sky. Warm but never glowing skin.

STRICTLY FORBIDDEN — no different face, no younger face, no open smile, no teeth, no beauty filter,
no airbrushed plastic skin, no added make-up, no changed eyebrows, no changed hair, no sunglasses
over the eyes, no cut-out look, no halo along her outline, no orange glow bath, no bloom, no lens
flare, no sun star, no extra limb, no missing or merged limb, no impossible hand, no floating hand,
no six-fingered hand, no text, no logo, no watermark, no border, no vignette, no HDR look, no
teal-and-orange grade, no dreamy soft focus. Real photographic grain, real contrast, vertical 4:5.
